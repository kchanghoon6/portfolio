System design · planned

Three layers, separate responsibilities

The engineering focus is reliable delivery across physical transitions: approaching an elevator, pressing and verifying a button, boarding safely, then discharging a package at the destination. Navigation, state verification and recovery must work together without relying on building integration. No boarding or delivery success rate has been measured.

Power & drive

Keep logic alive when motion stops

  • 12.8 V, 9.9 Ah LiFePO4 pack with fused branches. The MDD10A is reserved for traction: two motors per side, in parallel.
  • The emergency-stop circuit is intended to interrupt the relay coil and disconnect actuator power, independently of software STOP. Pi and ESP32 logic remain powered for fault handling.
  • Separate 5 V conversion supplies compute / sensors; a 6 V UBEC supplies servos. Actual output voltage, load current and protection behavior need bench verification.
  • Wheel supplier dimensions are Ø86.6 × 31 mm; nominal wheelbase is 330 mm. The approximately 21–22 mm unloaded clearance is a geometry estimate, not a demonstrated threshold capability.
Compute & sensing

Perception above, interlocks below

  • Pi 5: camera input, mission state, photo storage and transmission queue. ESP32-S3: local motion, encoder and switch processing.
  • Seven VL53L1X ToF sensors: three front, two side and two rear; Pi camera for forward perception and rear USB camera with lighting for delivery evidence.
  • INA226 current sensing, ramp limit / stow switches, IR break-beams and two latch-feedback inputs are planned checks, not installed proof of safe operation.
  • Pi–ESP32 USB communication is documented, but the ESP32 power route must be resolved to avoid overlapping supplies.
Cargo mechanism

Load from above, discharge behind

  • A fixed conveyor carries the package through a 200 mm guided corridor. The latest drawing’s package envelope is 140 × 200 × 100 mm and ≤2 kg, a design target, not a tested payload rating.
  • Two JK-XG-07A cabinet latches close mechanically and release with short 12 V pulses. The purchased latch variant’s feedback contacts still need physical verification.
  • Three worm-gear motors drive the belt and two ramp axes. A gas spring assists the ramp, with a servo-released plunger and stow switch for retention.
  • Gas-spring travel, motor-shaft engagement, friction and power-off holding loads remain verification gates.
Elevator & autonomy

A button press is only one step

  • A 2040 mast supports a DS3218 servo pushrod with spring compliance for button contact.
  • Button recognition, press confirmation, open-door detection and a clear boarding path are intended parts of the elevator workflow. Button-height adjustment and an optional extension axis are not finalized.
  • General door-opening hardware is outside the current scope.
  • Web ordering, checkpoint-based navigation and mission logging remain software plans, not claims of autonomous campus operation.

Status & planned validation

Design evidence, not test results

The current evidence is CAD, fabrication drawings and a revised wiring / assembly plan. Physical assembly, bench qualification and campus trials are still ahead.

Before energizing

Resolve the open electrical gates

  • Choose one ESP32 supply path and separate incompatible servo / motor PWM frequency needs.
  • Provide sufficient I/O: the documented switch, beam, latch and ToF reset allocation exceeds one 16-pin expander.
  • Finalize latch drive current margin and verify feedback behavior on the actual parts.
  • Detect actuator-bus loss and require deliberate re-arming. Releasing the emergency stop must not automatically restart motion.
Bench → controlled trials

Measure each transition

  • Check fit, fastener engagement, cable clearance, stability and ramp retention with actuator power removed.
  • Measure loaded currents and validate current-limit thresholds, emergency stop, timeouts and fault recovery before driving.
  • Test package exit, jams, latch / stow confirmation and no-repeat discharge under camera or network failure.
  • Then record repeated button-press, boarding and floor / destination checks, plus end-to-end delivery trials. No success-rate or payload result is reported yet.

Strength, motor torque, package friction, water resistance and tip stability are unvalidated. CAD checks do not establish physical safety or operational reliability.
